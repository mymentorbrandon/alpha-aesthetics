The Science

What Are MUSE Cells?

MUSE cells — short for Multilineage-differentiating Stress-Enduring cells — are a naturally occurring subset of cells first described by researchers in 2010. They are studied for their ability to survive stressful conditions and for characteristics that distinguish them from other cell populations found in connective tissue throughout the body.


Research in this area is ongoing, and the scientific understanding of MUSE cells continues to evolve. MUSE Cell FAQs

What does "MUSE cells" actually stand for?

Multilineage-differentiating Stress-Enduring cells. The name describes properties researchers have observed in these cells during laboratory study.

Is this the same as other stem cell terms I've heard?

No — there are several distinct categories of cells discussed in regenerative science, and the terminology matters. We're happy to walk through the differences during a consultation rather than oversimplify it here.
